What Is the PooreYouTuber Photo Studio Print Designer?

The PooreYouTuber Photo Studio Print Designer is a free, browser-based tool that turns one photograph into a print-ready sheet. You enter the physical size of the photo in millimeters, centimeters or inches, choose how many copies you need, and pick a paper size such as A4 or 4 × 6 inch photo paper. The tool arranges the copies with margins, spacing and optional cutting guides, then exports a PDF, JPG or PNG file.

It is designed for people who print photos regularly but do not want to learn desktop design software. Photo studios can prepare customer sheets quickly. Cyber cafes can produce photo prints for application forms. Students can prepare project photos, and families can print several copies of one picture on a single page.

Key Features of the Online Photo Studio Tool

The tool accepts custom dimensions in millimeters, centimeters and inches, and switching units keeps the physical size the same. Common preset sizes such as 35 × 45 mm and 65 × 95 mm are available and can be edited. The crop tool keeps the exact proportions of your chosen size, and you can zoom, drag, rotate, flip, and adjust brightness and contrast. The layout engine places 1, 4, 6, 8, 12 or 16 copies, or any custom count, on A4, A5, 4 × 6 inch or 5 × 7 inch paper, in portrait or landscape. Margins, horizontal and vertical spacing, and cutting guides are all adjustable. Export options include a multi-page PDF at the exact paper size, plus JPG and PNG images, and a quality report explains how sharp your print is likely to be.

How to Use the Photo Studio Print Designer

  1. Upload a photograph. Drag a JPG, PNG or WebP file into the upload area, or use Browse files. On a phone, pick an image from your gallery.
  2. Select a photo size. Choose a preset or type a width and height, and select your unit.
  3. Crop and adjust the photograph. Drag the photo to position it, use the zoom slider, then rotate, flip, brightness and contrast as needed. Press Apply when you are satisfied.
  4. Choose the paper size. Select A4, A5, 4 × 6 inch, 5 × 7 inch or a custom paper size, and set the orientation.
  5. Select the number of copies. Pick a preset count or enter your own.
  6. Adjust spacing, margins and cutting guides. Check the preview and the statistics panel.
  7. Review the quality report. Confirm the status before you export.
  8. Export the finished file. Choose PDF, JPG or PNG and download it.

How to Print Multiple Photos on A4 Paper

The layout engine works out how many photos fit by subtracting the margins from the paper size, then fitting as many photo widths and heights as possible with the spacing you set. For example, a 65 × 95 mm photo on A4 portrait (210 × 297 mm) with a 5 mm margin and 3 mm gaps has 200 mm of usable width, which fits two photos across. The usable height of 287 mm fits two rows. That gives four photos per sheet. A 35 × 45 mm photo on the same page fits five across and six down, which gives 30 photos. The tool shows these numbers before you export.

When you print, choose "Actual size" or 100% scale and turn off "Fit to page". Many printers scale documents automatically, and borderless printing can also shift the image. Print one test sheet on plain paper and measure a photo with a ruler before you print on photo paper.

Understanding Photo Size and 300 DPI

Physical size is measured in millimeters or inches, while digital images are measured in pixels. DPI (dots per inch) links the two. The formula is simple: pixels = physical size in inches × DPI. One inch equals 25.4 mm, so a 65 mm wide photo is 65 ÷ 25.4 = 2.56 inches. At 300 DPI that is about 768 pixels across. The 95 mm height is 3.74 inches, or about 1,122 pixels. A 35 × 45 mm photo at 300 DPI is roughly 413 × 531 pixels.

300 DPI does not restore detail that is missing from the original image. If your source photo is 400 pixels wide and you print it at 65 mm, the tool will report a low resolution because the image has fewer pixels than the output needs. Exporting at 300 DPI sets the file's resolution, but it cannot create new detail. A sharp, well-lit, high-resolution original gives the best result.

Privacy and Image Processing

Your photograph is read and processed on your own device using your browser's built-in image tools. The tool's code does not upload your photo to a server, a third-party image service or any external host. The only external file the page loads is the PDF library script used to create PDF files; that script is code, not your photo. The page does not contain analytics code that sends image contents or file names anywhere. When you close or reload the tab, the photo is removed from memory. If you use a browser extension or a hosting service that adds its own analytics, those are outside this tool's control.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I create a 65 × 95 mm photo?

Yes. Select the 65 × 95 mm preset, or enter 65 and 95 in millimeters. The output is generated at that exact physical size. Check the requirements of the institution you are submitting to, because this is a commonly used size and not an official standard.

Can I make 35 × 45 mm ID photos?

Yes. The 35 × 45 mm preset is included, and you can enter any custom size. As with every size here, confirm the exact specification required by your application.

Can I print 8 or 16 copies on one sheet?

Yes, both are available as presets, and you can also enter a custom count. The tool shows how many photos fit on the selected paper at your margin and spacing. If the number you request does not fit on one sheet, the layout uses additional sheets, and each one becomes a page in the PDF. Your photo size is never reduced to make it fit.

Can I create a layout for A4 or 4 × 6 paper?

Yes. A4, A5, 4 × 6 inch photo paper, 5 × 7 inch photo paper and custom paper sizes are all supported, in portrait or landscape orientation.

Can I download a PDF?

Yes. The PDF uses the exact physical size of the selected paper and places each sheet on its own page. Choose PDF in the export section and press Download file.

What does 300 DPI mean?

DPI stands for dots per inch. At 300 DPI, the output contains 300 pixels for every inch of printed length. For a 65 mm photo, that means about 768 pixels across. It is a common standard for photo printing.

Will a low-quality image become high quality after export?

No. The export can set the resolution you select, but it cannot add detail that the original image does not contain. The quality report compares your source resolution with the output you requested and shows whether the result will be good, acceptable or low.

Can I use the tool on a mobile phone?

Yes. The layout adapts to small screens, and you can upload from your phone's gallery. Downloads are handled by your browser, so the exact saving behaviour depends on your phone's browser and settings.

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. Processing happens in your browser on your own device. The tool does not include code that sends your photo to a server or an image-processing service.

Can I use the tool for free?

Yes. There is no login, subscription or payment required to use the Photo Studio Print Designer.
