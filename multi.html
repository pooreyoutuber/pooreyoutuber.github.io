What Is the Passport Photo Maker?

The PooreYouTuber Passport Photo Maker is a free, browser-based tool that turns an ordinary photograph into a passport-style photo and arranges copies on a printable sheet. You upload a picture from your phone or computer, adjust it with live controls, remove or replace the background, and choose the paper size and number of copies. The result can be downloaded as a print-ready PDF, JPG or PNG file.

The tool is made for people who need printed photos quickly: photo studios serving walk-in customers, cyber cafes preparing forms for applications, students preparing admission or exam documents, and families who want several prints of one picture. It does not require design software, an account or a payment.

Privacy: your photo is processed on your device and is not uploaded to a server by this tool.

Key Features of the Online Passport Photo Tool

The editor is organised as twelve numbered options. Photo size comes first, followed by crop and zoom, rotation and flipping, four tone controls (brightness, contrast, saturation and sharpness), black and white, auto enhance, background removal, background colour and a thin border. Every change appears in the live result immediately. The Back button undoes your last change, Forward redoes it, Reset returns the photo to its original state, and Clear removes the photo so you can start again. Ctrl+Z also works on a computer keyboard.

The layout engine works out how many photos fit on the chosen paper. It subtracts the margins, fits as many photo widths and heights as possible with the gaps you set, and centres the grid. If you ask for more photos than fit on one sheet, the tool does not shrink them. It creates extra sheets, and each sheet becomes one page in the PDF. The "Fill the sheet" button sets the count to the maximum that fits.

How to Make a Passport Photo Online

  1. Upload a photograph. Use Browse files, drag a picture onto the upload area, or pick one from your phone gallery. Use a photo that is sharp, well lit and taken from the front.
  2. Choose the photo size. Select a preset such as 35 × 45 mm, or type your own width and height in millimeters, centimeters or inches.
  3. Crop and zoom. Drag the photo in the preview until the face is where you want it, then use the zoom slider.
  4. Adjust the look. Use brightness, contrast, saturation and sharpness. Try auto enhance if the photo looks dull.
  5. Remove the background. Press Remove background and wait for the model to load the first time. Choose a background colour such as white or light blue.
  6. Add a border if you need cut lines. A thin border helps you see the edges of each photo after printing.
  7. Choose paper and copies. Select A4, B4, 4 × 6 or another paper, then 4, 8, 16 or a custom number of photos. Adjust margins and gaps if needed.
  8. Download. Choose PDF for printing at a print shop or JPG/PNG for other programs, then press Download file.

How to Print Multiple Passport Photos on A4 Paper

Printing several photos on one A4 sheet saves paper and gives you spares. The tool works from the physical size of the photo and the physical size of the paper. For example, a 35 × 45 mm photo on A4 portrait (210 × 297 mm) with a 5 mm margin and 2 mm gaps leaves 200 mm of usable width, which fits five photos across. The 287 mm usable height fits six rows. That gives thirty photos on one sheet. Choose "Fill the sheet" to use the full capacity, or pick a smaller number if you only need eight.

Print at actual size and turn off "Fit to page" in your printer dialog. Many printers scale a document unless told not to. Borderless printing can also shift the image slightly. Print one test copy on plain paper and measure a photo with a ruler before you print the full sheet on photo paper.

Understanding Photo Size, Pixels and 300 DPI

A physical size is measured in millimeters or inches, while a digital image is measured in pixels. DPI, or dots per inch, links the two. The formula is pixels = physical size in inches × DPI. One inch equals 25.4 mm. A 35 mm wide photo is 35 ÷ 25.4 = 1.38 inches, which is about 413 pixels at 300 DPI. A 45 mm height is 1.77 inches, or about 531 pixels. So a 35 × 45 mm photo at 300 DPI is roughly 413 × 531 pixels.

300 DPI does not add detail. It only tells the printer how many dots to place per inch. If your original photo is 400 pixels wide and you print it at 35 mm, the image has far fewer pixels than the 413 required, and the quality report will show a low status. Use the largest, sharpest original you have. Photos taken with modern phones usually have enough resolution for passport-size prints.

Background Removal and Background Colour

Background removal separates the person from the background using an AI segmentation model that runs in your browser. The result is a cut-out that can be placed on white, light blue, light grey, red or any custom colour. The method works best when the person is clearly separated from the background, faces the camera and is not blocked by other objects. Hair, glasses and busy backgrounds can produce soft or uneven edges, so check the preview and adjust the crop if needed.

Some offices require a plain white or light background. Others specify a colour or a certain style of photo. Always follow the specification given by the organisation that asked for the photo.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I make a passport photo online for free?

Yes. The tool has no login, subscription or payment step. You can crop, edit, remove the background and download the result without creating an account.

Can I create a 35 × 45 mm passport photo?

Yes. The 35 × 45 mm preset is included, and you can enter any custom size. Confirm the exact size and background that your application requires before printing.

Can I make a 65 × 95 mm or 2 × 2 inch photo?

Yes. Both are available as presets, and any custom size in millimeters, centimeters or inches works too.

Can I print 8 or 16 photos on one sheet?

Yes. Choose 8, 16 or a custom number in step 3. The tool shows how many fit on the selected paper. If your number does not fit on one sheet, the layout creates more sheets and tells you so.

Can I use A4, B4 or 4 × 6 paper?

Yes. A3, A4, B4, A5, B5, 4 × 6 inch and 5 × 7 inch photo paper are available, as is custom paper. You can select portrait or landscape orientation.

Can I download a PDF for printing?

Yes. The PDF uses the exact physical size of the selected paper, with one page per sheet. Choose PDF in step 4 and press Download file.

Does the tool remove the background automatically?

Yes, when you press Remove background. The AI model needs an internet connection the first time it loads. After that, the processing happens on your device. Results are best with a clear, front-facing portrait. You can change the background colour afterwards.

What does 300 DPI mean?

DPI means dots per inch. At 300 DPI the file contains 300 pixels for every inch of printed length, which is a common standard for photo printing.

Will a low-quality photo become sharp after export?

No. The export cannot add detail the original photo does not contain. The quality report shows whether your source is good, acceptable or low for the size and DPI you selected.

Can I use the tool on a mobile phone?

Yes. The layout adapts to small screens, and you can upload photos from your gallery. Download behaviour depends on your phone's browser.

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. Editing, background removal and PDF creation happen in your browser. The tool does not send your photo to a server.

Does an output from this tool meet official passport or visa requirements?

The tool provides common sizes and layout options, but it does not check any government or embassy specification. Requirements for passports, visas and ID documents differ by country and by document, and they can change. Always check the current official rules before you submit a photo.

Can I undo a mistake?

Yes. Press Back, or Ctrl+Z on a keyboard, to undo your last change. Press Forward to redo it. Reset all edits returns the photo to its original state.
